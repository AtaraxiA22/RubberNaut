Frost came in the night and signed everything but me. The pumpkins wear it like reconciliation — their candle work done, their faces gone soft and wise in daylight. The enclosure holds me at my own temperature, so the cold reads only on my breath: small white proof, there and gone. Polly writes a dark trail through the silvered grass, noses the broom by the door, forgives the morning its brightness. The hat is on its peg until next year. I carried the last pumpkin down myself — no ceremony, just two hands and a thank you. Some seasons you sweep out; some you simply let go cold. Gus watches the frost from the correct side of the window. Cocoa stands ready. Witch's honor.
